## 摘要

Nutrient availability in soil is temporally and spatially heterogeneous, and, as a result, microbial migration is critical for many species. The nature of microbial movement in soil, however, is unknown due to a lack of observations and experimental data. We developed live-imaging and image-analysis techniques to track the movement of single cells through soil to elucidate how Bacillus subtilis utilises pore space during the early root colonisation. The study reveals that the bacterium can modify fluid pathways to create streams, even at low bulk cell density. The phenomenon was influenced by pore structure, distance from the root and the viscosity of the soil solution. By generating macroscopic fluid motion, bacteria may also be able to travel faster and farther than individually, while limiting energy expenditure.
