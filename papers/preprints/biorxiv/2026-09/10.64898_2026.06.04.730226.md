## 摘要

Light supports circadian regulation and is associated with non-communicable diseases, yet ocular exposure patterns remain poorly understood. We recruited 191 adults across nine sites in seven countries, combining diaries and contextual reports with near-eye (141 participants; 816 participant-days) and complementary chest-level measurements (154 participants; 902 participant-days). In near-eye analyses, only 24.0% of recorded daytime minutes met the recommendation of at least 250 lx melanopic equivalent daylight illuminance; 63.3% of pre-sleep and 87.7% of bedside sleep-environment minutes met their respective limits. Variation among people and days within sites exceeded that among sites (ratio 1.99; 95%-CI, 1.29-4.77). In hourly models, light source and setting contributed the largest shares of fitted variation after time of day. Site-average outdoor exposure was 9.35 times that while awake at home (95%-CI, 6.95-12.59; 714.2 versus 76.4 lx). This baseline identifies a daytime exposure gap and supports testing interventions in everyday settings to improve light exposure and health.
