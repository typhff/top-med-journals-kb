# Sequential assembly in vivo of the bacteriophage P22 genome translocation channel

## 摘要

Bacteriophage P22 ejects four internal proteins into infected cells to assemble a channel for genome translocation across the cell envelope. However, how these proteins cooperate to build the trans-envelope channel remains enigmatic. Using in situ single-particle cryogenic electron microscopy, we determined near-atomic in vivo structures of P22 infection intermediates, revealing how the tail hub protein gp10 templates the sequential assembly of three internal proteins gp7*, gp20 and gp16 into a ~50-nm trans-envelope channel that extends from the virion into the host cytoplasm. Functional analyses explain why phage DNA can be detected in supernatant fluids during infection by channel-defective mutants. Our structures also provide a mechanistic basis for the long-standing observation that a P22 virion lacking gp16 can be complemented, extra-cytoplasmically, by a co-infecting virion lacking gp20, thereby allowing the genome in the gp16-deficient particle to enter the host cytoplasm.
