## 摘要

How tumour genotype reshapes systemic metabolism to drive immune tolerance and tumour progression remains unclear. Here, we show that Pten-deficient Drosophila tumours reprogram host tryptophan metabolism, triggering systemic and local changes in immune and stromal compartments to foster tolerance. Tumour-derived nitric oxide activates the kynurenine pathway in liver-like tissue, selectively increasing the 3-hydroxykynurenine (3-HK) branch. Host 3-HK promotes tumour progression and suppresses immune function via aryl hydrocarbon receptor (AhR) signalling. These results identify the kynurenine pathway as a central tumour-host nexus and reveal how tumour genotype creates immune vulnerabilities.
