# Cancer-associated nucleolar morphological changes coincide with altered subcompartment organization and accelerated nascent rRNA trafficking in breast cancer cells

## 摘要

Nucleolar alterations are characteristic features of cancer cells; however, how changes in the overall morphology relate to internal subcompartment organization and ribosomal RNA (rRNA) dynamics remains unclear. In this study, the nucleolar organization of non-transformed MCF-10A mammary epithelial cells and MDA-MB-231 breast cancer cells was quantitatively compared. MDA-MB-231 cells exhibited differences in nucleolar morphology, accompanied by altered spatial organization of the dense fibrillar component and granular component, and accelerated intranucleolar trafficking of nascent rRNA. Pharmacological perturbation with SU056 shifted multiple structural and dynamic parameters toward an MCF-10A-like profile. Conversely, transient physicochemical perturbation with 1,6-hexanediol induced several MDA-MB-231-like nucleolar features in MCF-10A cells, which returned to their untreated profile after washout. These findings support the view that cancer-associated nucleolar alterations are an integrated reorganization across morphological, spatial, and dynamic dimensions. The observed reversibility further indicates that cancer-associated nucleolar organization can be remodeled in response to perturbations. By linking the morphology, subcompartment organization, and nascent rRNA dynamics within a common framework, this study provides a basis for exploring the establishment, maintenance, and remodeling of distinct nucleolar profiles in cancer.
