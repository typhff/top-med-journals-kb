## 摘要

T cells are major contributors to protective immunity against pathogens and cancers. Immune memory is a central feature of protective adaptive immunity, and mucosal barrier tissues are major sites of pathogen invasion. Yet, fundamental gaps remain in our knowledge of human T cell immune memory durability in mucosal barrier sites. Here, we employed minimally invasive nasal swab sampling to directly detect, and longitudinally assess, human antigen-specific T cells in two upper airway tissue sites (~ 900 samples) and peripheral blood. Antigen-specific memory CD8 and CD4 T cells were detected in upper airway tissue of > 95% of individuals. Both memory CD8 and CD4 T cells were sustained in both mucosal epithelial and mucosal lymphoid tissue over the course of 18+ months, with monthly sampling, and no clear evidence of decline. Virus-specific upper airway CD8 and CD4 T cells were predominantly resident memory T cells (TRM) throughout the 18+ month period of observation. These findings can inform future T cell vaccines and therapeutics.
