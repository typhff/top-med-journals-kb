# A structural census links penultimate-residue class to N-terminal burial in human protein assemblies

## 摘要

Initiator-methionine excision is among the earliest protein modifications, yet its relationship to assembly geometry is unknown. Burial of the mature first residue was measured across 7,246 deposited human biological assemblies (22,291 chain-level observations; 1,191 proteins). Among 1,143 analyzable proteins, termini in MetAP-permissive penultimate-residue sequence classes were less often interface-engaged than termini in MetAP-nonpermissive classes (37.4% versus 47.4%; adjusted odds ratio 0.65, p = 7.2e-4). Curated processing annotations did not show a corresponding burial difference, and correlated residue properties preclude attributing the sequence-class association specifically to iMet removal. The analysis identified 264 interface-engaged MetAP-permissive candidates concentrated in cellular machines. In a fully recomputed conformer scan of deeply buried proteasome positions, modeled methionine accommodation was less favorable than at observed-methionine controls (median overlap -0.30 versus -1.12 angstrom, p = 0.0049), although most scoreable sites permitted a nonoverlapping placement. The census therefore reveals a graded structural constraint - not universal steric failure - and prioritizes complexes in which altered packing, assembly kinetics, lipidation or N-terminal methylation can be tested.
