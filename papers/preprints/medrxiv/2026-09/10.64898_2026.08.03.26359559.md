# Adults who suspect they may be autistic or have ADHD show corresponding neurodevelopmental polygenic effects

## 摘要

Approximately 3.4% of UK adults suspect they may be autistic and 5.8% suspect they may have Attention Deficit Hyperactive Disorder (ADHD) without a formal diagnosis. Whether this reflects the polygenic architecture of the corresponding neurodevelopmental conditions or a broader mix of other psychiatric conditions is unknown. We analysed UK Biobank participants who completed autism (n=154,926) and ADHD (n=161,623) trait questionnaires, classifying them into no diagnosis, suspected, or diagnosed groups from a self-report question. We conducted genome-wide association studies of self-reported suspected autism (n=6,797) and suspected ADHD (n=3,611), estimated genetic correlations with neurodevelopmental and psychiatric conditions, and characterised polygenic score (PGS) and co-occurring mental health profiles across groups. Genetic correlations between self-reported suspected autism or ADHD and external autism and ADHD GWAS were not statistically different from 1, and were higher for later-diagnosed autism and adulthood-diagnosed ADHD than childhood-diagnosed forms; correlations with other psychiatric conditions were low to moderate. PGS for most neurodevelopmental and mental health conditions were similarly elevated in self-reported suspected and diagnosed groups relative to no diagnosis, but co-occurring mental health conditions and neurodevelopmental trait scores were highest in the self-reported diagnosed group and intermediate in the self-reported suspected group. Within the self-reported suspected group, PGS and psychiatric diagnoses increased with increasing trait scores. These findings indicate that, as a group, adults who self-report that they have suspected undiagnosed autism or ADHD show a genetic correlation pattern more specific to the corresponding condition than to general psychiatric liability.
