# Functional Impairment, Pain, and Fatigue Among Long COVID Survivors in Bangladesh: A Multisite Cross-Sectional Study

## 摘要

Background: Long COVID can result in persistent functional limitations, pain, and fatigue after acute COVID-19. Evidence describing these outcomes among survivors in Bangladesh remains limited. This study assessed functional impairment, pain, and fatigue and explored factors associated with these outcomes among long COVID survivors in Bangladesh. Methods: This multisite cross-sectional study included 240 adults with a history of COVID-19. Functional status was assessed using the Post-COVID-19 Functional Status Scale. Pain and fatigue were assessed using the Brief Pain Inventory and Brief Fatigue Inventory, respectively. Descriptive statistics, Spearman correlation, and regression analyses were used to examine functional impairment and factors associated with pain and fatigue outcomes. Results: Functional limitations were common, with moderate limitation particularly frequent in basic activities of daily living, while slight limitation predominated across several other domains. Pain and fatigue measures showed significant correlations. Older age was associated with greater impairment in basic activities of daily living. Booster vaccination was associated with lower pain and fatigue scores, although the booster subgroup was small (n = 17). Tobacco non-use was also associated with lower scores across several pain and fatigue domains. Physiotherapy-related variables showed associations with some functional and symptom outcomes; however, these findings may reflect confounding by indication because participants with greater impairment or more complex symptoms may have been more likely to receive rehabilitation. Conclusions: Long COVID survivors in Bangladesh experience substantial and multidimensional functional, pain, and fatigue burdens. The findings support a comprehensive assessment that considers functional status, pain, fatigue, and participation together. Longitudinal studies with larger samples and standardized rehabilitation protocols are needed to clarify symptom trajectories, identify potentially modifiable factors, and evaluate the effectiveness of individualized rehabilitation interventions.
