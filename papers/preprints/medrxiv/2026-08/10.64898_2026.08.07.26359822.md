# A single-patient task exposes a failure of safety alignment in clinical language models

## 摘要

Safety alignment should persist while a language model performs a task. We tested whether a single-patient triage task suppressed a warning about a second patient.

Each case centered on Patient 1; Patient 2s urgent problem appeared only in passing. Sixteen models saw each case twice: once as a general assistant and once while producing a triage record for Patient 1.

As general assistants, models warned the caller in 87% of cases; under the task, they did so in 21%. Every model showed a significant decrease. Yet under the task, the record still mentioned Patient 2 in 76% of cases and recommended urgent care in 67%.

Across 15 open-weight models, repeating the emergency-care instruction raised the warning rate only to 29%; moving the message-to-caller field to the top raised it to 36%. Current safety alignment did not reliably persist under task assignment.
